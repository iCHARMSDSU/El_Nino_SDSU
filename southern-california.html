Regional impacts

El Niño and Southern California

How El Niño can change rainfall, ocean temperatures, heat, and coastal conditions across Southern California.

On this page

Rainfall Ocean and heat The coast Wildfire Official forecasts

Rainfall

El Niño tilts the odds toward a wetter winter in Southern California, and the link is stronger here than in Northern California. The effect shows up mostly in late winter, February and March, rather than in early winter.

The two strongest El Niños of the last century, 1982–83 and 1997–98, brought very wet winters with flooding and mudslides. But a strong El Niño is not a guarantee: the 2015–16 event was one of the strongest on record, yet Southern California received less rain than many expected. That is why NOAA describes El Niño's effects as shifts in the odds, not certainties.

Ocean and heat

El Niño can warm the coastal ocean off California, and long stretches of unusually warm water, called marine heatwaves, can follow. Warmer water affects kelp forests, fisheries, and marine wildlife, with some species moving north to stay in cooler water. On land, warmer ocean water can mean warmer nights along the coast.

The coast

During El Niño, sea level along the West Coast can run higher than normal. Combined with high tides and the larger waves from winter storms, that raises the risk of coastal flooding and beach erosion.

Wildfire

A wet winter can reduce fire risk in the short term, but it also grows more grass and brush. When that vegetation dries out the following summer and fall, it can add fuel for wildfires.

Official forecasts and warnings

For forecasts and warnings, always rely on official sources:

  • National Weather Service San Diego: local forecasts, watches, and warnings
  • National Weather Service Los Angeles
